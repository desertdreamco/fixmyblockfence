Block Wall Footing-Related Repair & Reconstruction

A block wall can show visible symptoms above grade even when the problem involves conditions at the base. Settlement, erosion, exposed or damaged footing conditions and movement around pilasters can change the repair scope. We evaluate the wall as a system rather than covering the visible crack.

What we look for

Visible conditions that can change the repair scope.

  • Movement concentrated near the wall base
  • Exposed or undermined footing areas
  • Settlement-related cracking
  • Leaning associated with base movement
  • Erosion next to the wall
  • Failed sections requiring footing and masonry reconstruction

Our process

How we determine the right repair scope.

1

Photograph the wall base and any exposed footing conditions

2

Review the crack and movement pattern above the affected area

3

Determine how much of the wall must be removed to perform the repair correctly

4

Reconstruct the affected masonry and base-related scope as required

5

Recommend engineering involvement when the condition exceeds a contractor-level repair assessment

Questions

Common questions about block wall footing-related repair & reconstruction.

Can you tell from photos if the footing failed?

Photos can help identify visible clues, but concealed footing conditions may require on-site evaluation and sometimes engineering review.

Do all wall cracks come from the footing?

No. Cracks can have many causes, which is why the pattern and surrounding conditions matter.

Do you provide structural engineering?

No. Desert Dreamco performs contractor repair evaluations and masonry work. We may recommend a structural engineer when appropriate.
